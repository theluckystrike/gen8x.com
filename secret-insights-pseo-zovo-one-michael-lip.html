02 / The 10 Rules

Never Break These Again

Each rule is extracted from a specific mistake that cost real traffic and real money. Ordered by damage caused.

1
Never ship more than 50 pages per week on a domain under DR 30
We shipped 3,760 pages in 30 days on a DR 5 domain. Google flagged it as programmatic spam. The velocity threshold scales with domain authority. New domain (<6 months): 10-20 pages/week. DR 20+: 50/week. DR 50+: no practical limit.
2
Never change more than 200 files in a single commit
One sprint changed 3,647 files. Google interprets massive simultaneous changes as "this site was rebuilt by a bot." Normal healthy sites change 5-50 files per deploy. Split batches with 48 hours between pushes.
3
48 hours minimum between deploys
We had 10 commits in 48 hours. Each deploy triggers a Google re-crawl. Rapid re-crawls on changing content equals a "content churn" signal. Deploy once, wait 48 hours, verify indexation, then deploy again.
4
Measure before expanding. Always.
We shipped 14 sprints without measurement data between ANY of them. Not one sprint was designed from data produced by the previous sprint. Every sprint must have a Day 7 checkpoint before the next sprint ships.
5
Template articles are a liability, not an asset
"[Tool] for [X] Workflow Guide" x 200 creates a pattern Google's Information Gain scoring penalizes. Same structure, same word count, same CTA placement. Ship 50 excellent pages, not 500 template pages.
6
Build failures during Googlebot crawls are catastrophic
Multiple builds failed due to syntax errors. The site served stale content or 404s when Googlebot visited. Every deploy must be verified live within 5 minutes. A broken build serving 404s for even 2 hours can cause pages to drop.
7
Interactive tools survive. Articles don't.
AI Overviews destroyed 61% of informational CTR in 2026. Template articles are replaceable by AI summaries. Interactive tools (calculators, generators, checkers) are not. Tools are the moat. This is exactly the approach we use at zovo.one.
8
Domain authority is earned over months, not days
We went from 0 to 3,760 pages in 30 days on a brand-new domain. A 2-year-old DR 40 site could ship the same pages and be rewarded. Age + gradual growth = trust. New + explosive growth = spam signal.
9
Distribution doesn't need Google
Two customers paid $99 each during the traffic crash. Neither came from organic search. Both came from direct links. Google is one distribution channel. HN, Reddit, Twitter, Dev.to, Discord, email are others.
10
Product-market fit is proven when people pay despite broken infrastructure
$198 in revenue during a 96% traffic crash. Edge functions undeployed for 36 hours. CTAs pointing to 404s. Despite all of this, 3 people found the product and paid $99. If people pay when everything is broken, the product works.

04 / What Worked

Keep These Patterns

✓
Interactive tools with localStorage paywall
10 vanilla HTML/JS tools. Zero dependencies. 2 free uses, then blur + CTA. Under 120KB each. AI Overview-proof. These are the highest-quality assets we produced. We now build every zovo.one tool this way.
✓
Machine-verified QA gates (binary PASS/FAIL)
Gates are PASS or FAIL. Never WARN. Machine-measured with grep, wc, curl. Never self-reported by agents. If the report can't produce raw numbers, the gate validator doesn't exist.
✓
Authentic first-person copy converts
"I run 5 AI subscriptions from a laptop in Da Nang" converted better than generic marketing copy. Developers trust developers. Real credentials are facts no competitor can replicate.
✓
$99 Lifetime converts from cold traffic
Three customers in 9 days. Two at $99 from payment links, not Google. The product and price point work. The problem was never the offer — it was the distribution channel getting cut.

05 / Pre-Deploy Checklist

Run This Before Every Content Push

!
Is this commit under 200 files? If not, split into batches with 48h between each.
!
Has it been 48+ hours since the last deploy? If not, wait.
!
Did I measure the previous sprint before designing this one? If not, pull GSC data first.
!
Does the local build pass with zero errors? Verify before pushing.
!
Am I shipping fewer than 50 new pages this week? Count total new pages across ALL commits.
~
Does every new page add unique information? If it's a template + keyword swap, don't ship it.
~
Did I verify 5 random URLs return HTTP 200 within 5 minutes of deploy?
+
Would this page be useful if Google disappeared tomorrow? If not, make it interactive.
06 / Migration Protocol

When Moving Content to a New Domain

A
Do NOT 301 redirect from a penalized domain
Google can transfer penalty signals through 301 redirects. Clean break. No redirects. No links between old and new domains. Let the new domain build trust independently.
B
Migrate 10 pages on day one. Then 20/week.
Total migration to 200 best pages takes 8-10 weeks, not 8-10 hours. The patience IS the strategy.
C
Only migrate proven content
From 3,760 articles, only ~200 are worth migrating. The keyword pages, tools, and funnel articles that actually had clicks. Don't bring the problem to the new domain.
D
Deindex on old domain before indexing on new
Duplicate content across two domains means both get demoted. One page, one domain, one index entry.
07 / The Framework

Apply This to Every Future pSEO Project

I
Phase 1: Foundation (Week 1-2)
10 pages maximum. Homepage + best content + tools. Payment and attribution infrastructure. Do not expand until these 10 pages are indexed and getting impressions.
II
Phase 2: Validation (Week 3-6)
20 pages/week. Measure after each batch. If indexation rate drops below 80%, stop and diagnose. Total at end: ~100 pages.
III
Phase 3: Scaling (Week 7+)
Only if Phase 2 metrics are healthy. 50 pages/week max under DR 30. Every page must pass the Information Gain test.
IV
Continuous: Distribution is not optional
If 100% of your traffic comes from one source, you have zero traffic the day that source cuts you off. Distribute from day 1. One post per day to one channel. Post first, sprint second.
